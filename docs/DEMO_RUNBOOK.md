# CivicNexus BRICS — 3–5 Minute Demo Runbook

## Opening: 0:00–0:30

Open the public page. State the problem in one sentence: fragmented, multilingual feedback makes local infrastructure demand difficult to compare and act upon across BRICS contexts. Then introduce CivicNexus as a transparent bridge from citizen signal to human-reviewed policy action.

> “CivicNexus does not automate public policy. It makes citizen needs structured, multilingual, comparable, and auditable before a human decision is made.”

## Citizen signal: 0:30–1:30

Open **Submit a signal**. Switch the form to a non-English language, select an infrastructure category, select `high` or `critical` urgency, and describe a locally meaningful infrastructure gap. Pin a map location if the Google Maps layer is available. If the environment blocks map loading, use the visible manual locality, latitude, and longitude fallback.

Explain that the system records the exact citizen-selected category and urgency rather than overwriting them. On submission, the platform saves the request first. It then runs schema-constrained Gemini analysis and records an owner-only high-urgency alert when applicable.

## Trust trace: 1:30–2:10

Open the protected request trace. Highlight the structured classification, AI confidence, urgency score, evidence statements, cross-border themes, and translated view selector. Emphasise that the output is advisory, reviewable, and stored separately from the citizen’s original language.

Show the four-stage status pipeline: `submitted → reviewed → prioritized → actioned`.

## Decision support: 2:10–3:25

Switch to an account assigned `policymaker` or `admin` and open **Policy board**. Explain the three score components:

| Score | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Impact | Combines citizen-selected urgency, AI urgency, request concentration, and country reach. |
| Alignment | Shows how widely a category spans the five BRICS country contexts. |
| Priority | Transparent composite of impact, alignment, and model confidence. |

Select a priority, generate a decision brief, change its language, and export a PDF. Point out that brief readiness is the second owner-only notification trigger.

## Oversight: 3:25–4:10

Open **Moderation** as an administrator. Advance a request through the allowed status progression. Show the audit record generated for each action. Close with the principle that every public-facing recommendation is subject to human validation, local governance, and independently verified outcomes.

## Closing: 4:10–4:30

Conclude with the implementation value: a multilingual Digital Public Good pattern that can be piloted city by city, connected to verified data sources, and governed by the institutions responsible for delivery.
